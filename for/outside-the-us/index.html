Who it's for

Organizations outside the United States

Talus GIS LLC is a US company, and Talus is built to run anywhere. A town, a district, a university group, or an NGO on another continent installs the same release on its own server, keeps its data in its own country, and owes nobody a subscription. This page says what that looks like today, and what is not here yet.

What it does for you

Your server, your country, your data. Talus runs on one host you control. Nothing phones home: no telemetry, no update checker, no license server. Two conveniences are on when you install and reach the internet: a public address search, and street maps that each viewer's browser fetches straight from OpenStreetMap, which then sees the viewer's internet address and the area being viewed. An administrator can switch both off; download your area once and the maps draw from your own server. With those off and no other outbound connection configured, the stack makes none, which you can confirm at your own network layer. Data residency is wherever you put the machine.

Any coordinate system. Layers carry their own storage CRS, imports reproject on the way in with a preview, and exports reproject on the way out. National grids, UTM zones, and local projections are ordinary.

Your identity provider and your field crews. Staff sign in through any SAML or OIDC provider, with local accounts for a crew without a directory entry. Field collection uses QField, which is free, open source, and already used worldwide; a Talus publisher generates the field project in one click.

No credits, no meters, no surprise bills. Talus Community is free under the AGPL with no per-seat count. Talus+ is one flat, public price per deployment; the AI assists carry an included allowance and pause at the ceiling rather than billing past it.

What you need

One machine with Docker, or a virtual machine on the server you already run; four vCPUs and 8 GB are comfortable for a small organization. The where it runs section covers each option. The install and every runbook are in the repository.

English, for now. The portal interface and the documentation are in English today. A team that works in English can run it; a localized interface is not here yet, and this page will say so until it is.

Working hours. Talus Community questions and bug reports go by email and are read by a person, with no time commitment. Talus+ support is by email on a published schedule in a US time zone; it commits to a response time, not a resolution time, and says so on the support page. Talus+ quotes are in US dollars and payable by card or bank transfer.

What it deliberately does not do

No localized interface or translated documentation yet. No hosted offering: every Talus deployment is self-hosted, so a residency requirement is met by where you put the server. No certifications: no ISO 27001, no national government accreditation; self-hosting keeps your data inside your own boundary, and that is the whole compliance answer Talus can give today. One default to change if your rules cover visitors' internet addresses: set map backgrounds to Downloaded, so no viewer's browser contacts a map publisher. No 3D, routing, utility network, or heavy imagery analytics, anywhere.

Talus GIS has the same open core everywhere: the AGPL cannot be withdrawn, the installed software never stops working, and the exit is the same installer you came in with.
